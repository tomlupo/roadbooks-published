Roadbook

Ten road stages for two weeks in the heart of the Serra de Tramuntana — every one from the door in Sóller, no car needed, no flat kilometre wasted (there are none). Every line is road-exact, routed by a bicycle router (BRouter, low-traffic profile) over OpenStreetMap, and every map is a real map: pan, zoom, street-level detail. Each GPX carries turn cues and supply waypoints and imports into Strava's route builder, komoot or your head unit as-is.

THE climb from this side of the island: Puig Major, 14 km at 6% from your doorstep to the tunnel at 850 m — the highest road point on Mallorca — then through to the Cúber and Gorg Blau lakes under the radar domes. Turn at the second dam and enjoy the descent you earned.

Sóller → Puig Major → tunnel → Cúber → Gorg Blau → return

The tunnel is short and lit but bring a rear light; the lakes plateau is exposed to wind.

Sa Calobra the honest way: over Puig Major from your own door, past both lakes to Coll dels Reis, down the 26 hairpins and the tie-knot loop to the sea — and then the double payment: 700 m back up Sa Calobra AND the climb back to the tunnel before the long drop home. 73 km with 2,000 m of climbing. The queen stage of this book.

Sóller → Puig Major → Gorg Blau → Coll dels Reis → Port de sa Calobra → climb back → tunnel → Sóller

Dawn start, two bottles, refill at the port and Gorg Blau junction. After 10:30 the hairpins are a car park.

The grand tour: over the top past the lakes to Lluc's monastery, down Sa Batalla's hairpins to Caimari, then the long false-flat of the plain through Inca and Santa Maria — and the sting in the tail, Coll de Sóller from Bunyola, when your legs are already spent. Lunch in Caimari or Inca; the plain can be a furnace — load bottles before leaving Lluc.

Honest notes

  • Routing. Computed 2026-08-05 with BRouter's fastbike-lowtraffic profile over OpenStreetMap; distances and ascent from the router's geometry and elevation model. Short waypoint artifacts trimmed. Roadworks and closures it cannot know — glance at the line before you ride.
  • There is no flat here. The shortest spin carries 150 m of climbing; "recovery" in Sóller means gears, not speed. Pace estimates already account for it.
  • Surface & traffic are audited, not assumed. Every metre carries its OSM tags; the per-stage line is computed from them. The Ma-10 is the island's cycling artery — busy with bikes and, in season, cars around Deià and Valldemossa: mornings are golden.
  • The GPX navigates. Turn cues plus café, water and bike-shop waypoints from OpenStreetMap. The maps are embedded down to street level and work offline.
  • Heat and water. Long waterless stretches on the Puig Major side — the Gorg Blau junction café and the Sa Calobra port are the only refills on stage 9. Bottles full at every chance.
  • Segments. Swept via Strava Segment Explore and your starred list; named climbs verified against climbfinder the day of writing.
  • Pace numbers. "At your pace" uses your Strava month — 25–27 km/h flat, ~90 rpm — plus half a minute per 10 m climbed. Tramuntana days will run long; that's the point.
