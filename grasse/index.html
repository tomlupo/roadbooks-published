The shortest thing in the book and the one to ride if the legs are stale or the light is going: down off the Grasse shelf to Peymeinade, west to Le Tignet, then the Siagne side through Auribeau and Pégomas before the climb back up. Be honest with yourself about that last part — every ride from this door finishes by regaining the 250 m you gave away in the first ten minutes.

Door → Peymeinade → Le Tignet → Auribeau-sur-Siagne → Pégomas → Door

Nothing around Grasse is flat: this is the shortest stage here and it still carries 678 m of climbing. Treat it as an hour and a half, not a recovery spin.

Not a loop — the ride back from the bike shop. Out of Golfe-Juan and straight off the seafront, up through Vallauris, over the Mougins ridge, then the good part: Valbonne's grid of plane trees, Opio, Châteauneuf, and the long drag up to the Grasse shelf. The scenic-and-rolling day, and the quietest stage in the book — two thirds of it on lanes, and barely 2 km of anything you would call a main road. Grasse to Châteauneuf, then the perched-village belt east through Opio, Le Rouret, Valbonne and Mouans-Sartoux, and home up the Siagne through Pégomas and Auribeau. Best coffee stop of the eight is Valbonne's arcaded square, roughly halfway round. On Sunday mornings these roads fill with local club riders — that is a feature, not a warning.

Montée de Gourdon

FROM THE DOORCLIMB DAY

1,201m ↑
3h12at your pace
747m high point

100.0% paved · 34.1% quiet lanes · 3.9 km on N-roads

The Gourdon climb on its own, for a day when you want the famous one without the full four hours. Roll east to Châteauneuf and Le Bar-sur-Loup, drop to Pont du Loup, then turn up the D3 and climb the balcony to Gourdon — a village welded onto a cliff 500 m above the river, with the whole coast laid out behind you. Turn at the village, take the same balcony back down, and go home the way you came.

Door → Châteauneuf-Grasse → Le Bar-sur-Loup → Pont du Loup → Gourdon → Pont du Loup → Le Bar-sur-Loup → Châteauneuf-Grasse → Door

The least quiet stage here — only a third of it is on lanes, because the Grasse–Châteauneuf–Pont du Loup corridor is the D2085/D3 and it is the region's through-route. Go early. The Gourdon balcony itself is worth every metre of the approach.

The one day that leaves the limestone behind. Down the Siagne to Pégomas, then up into the Tanneron massif — the mimosa hills, red earth and umbrella pines instead of white rock — and over the top to the Lac de Saint-Cassien, crossing the water on the Pont du Pré Claou. Home along the Siagne valley and back up the shelf. Completely different country from everything else in this book.

Door → Peymeinade → Le Tignet → Pégomas → Tanneron → Pont du Pré Claou → Le Tignet → Peymeinade → Door

The busiest stage in the book by a distance: 18 km of it runs on the D562/D2562 primary, the Grasse–Draguignan artery. Wide and well surfaced, but it is not a lane. If traffic is the thing you hate most, ride stage 4 instead.

Gorges du Loup & Col de l'Écre

FROM THE DOORQUEEN STAGE
67.5km
1,582m ↑
4h01at your pace

100.0% paved · 46.2% quiet lanes · 3.9 km on N-roads

The one you came for. East to Châteauneuf and Le Bar-sur-Loup, down to Pont du Loup, then straight into the Gorges du Loup on the D6 — a shelf cut into the gorge wall, tunnels, the Cascade de Courmes falling onto the road, and almost no traffic because the road goes nowhere fast. Up to Bramafan, then the D3 ramp onto the Col de l'Écre at 1118 m. The reward is the descent: over the top and down the Gourdon balcony with the whole Côte d'Azur in front of you the entire way.

Door → Châteauneuf-Grasse → Le Bar-sur-Loup → Pont du Loup → Gorges du Loup → Cascade de Courmes → Bramafan → Col de l'Écre → Gourdon → Pont du Loup → Le Bar-sur-Loup → Châteauneuf-Grasse → Door

Ride it in this direction. Up the gorge is shaded and steady; the Gourdon side is the one you want to descend with the view, not stare at your stem on. The gorge road is narrow with rock overhangs and a couple of unlit tunnels — a rear light is not optional. Nothing open for food between Pont du Loup and Gourdon, so carry it.

The same gorge and the same col, but instead of turning for home at the top you carry west across the Plateau de Caussols — high limestone desert, dry-stone walls, sheep, the Calern observatory domes on the skyline, and a silence that is genuinely startling this close to Cannes. Over the Col du Ferrier at 1039 m, down to Saint-Vallier-de-Thiey, then home over Cabris and Spéracèdes. The high point of the book at 1144 m.

Door → Châteauneuf-Grasse → Le Bar-sur-Loup → Pont du Loup → Gorges du Loup → Bramafan → Col de l'Écre → Caussols → Col du Ferrier → Saint-Vallier-de-Thiey → Cabris → Spéracèdes → Peymeinade → Door

Harder than stage 7 in every way except the ascent number: the plateau is exposed, there is no shelter and no shop between Gourdon and Saint-Vallier, and the wind up there does what it likes. Two bottles minimum. If it is blowing, take stage 7 instead.

If you end up with a morning near the shop, this is what the coast is for. Round the bay through Cannes and La Napoule, then onto the Corniche d'Or where the Esterel drops its red porphyry straight into the sea — Théoule, the Esquillon headland, Miramar. Turn at Miramar and take the same road back, which is no hardship: the view works in both directions and the light is better coming home.

Golfe-Juan → Cannes → La Napoule → Théoule-sur-Mer → Miramar → Théoule-sur-Mer → La Napoule → Cannes → Golfe-Juan

Be clear-eyed about this one: it is the least quiet stage in the book at 27%, because the Corniche d'Or is a primary road and there is no alternative — it is the only road along that coast. It is also the flattest thing here by a mile. Ride it early on a weekday morning and it is glorious; ride it at midday in August and you will spend the day in traffic.

Collect the bike and ride the queen stage in one continuous line, without doubling back to the door first. Honest notes

  • Routing. Computed 2026-08-23 with BRouter's fastbike-lowtraffic profile over OpenStreetMap — it prefers paved back roads to the car-optimal line. Distances and ascent come from the router's own geometry and elevation model (OSM + SRTM), not from an estimate; ascent is the positive sum after smoothing, calibrated against BRouter's filtered ascend. Roadworks and closures it cannot know — glance at the line before you ride.
  • Every waypoint is a real coordinate. Villages, cols and the lake crossing were taken from OpenStreetMap via Overpass (place, mountain_pass and natural=peak nodes) rather than from a free-text geocoder, which in this region will cheerfully hand you a street in Cannes when you ask for a col. Two pins — Peymeinade and Opio — sat just off their through-roads on the first pass and sent the route down a 422 m gravel path to reach them; both were snapped onto the road and the gravel disappeared.
  • Surface is audited, not assumed. Every metre carries its OpenStreetMap tags through the router and the per-stage paved figure is computed from them. All thirteen stages come out at 99.8% paved or better, and eight of them at a clean 100% — the residual metres are untagged village-square and footway connectors, not riding surface. Untagged rural lanes are assumed paved. Nothing in this book asks for more than 25 mm.
  • Traffic is measured too. The 'quiet' figure on each card is the share of the stage that is not on a primary or secondary road. It runs from 67% on stage 3 down to 27% on stage 10, where the Corniche d'Or is the only road along that coast and 32 of its 45 km are primary. Stage 6 carries 18 km of primary road, and the Grasse–Pont du Loup corridor used by stages 5, 7, 12 and 13 is the region's through-route with no lane alternative. Those are the ones to start early.
  • Climbs. The named climbs were scraped from their climbfinder pages on 2026-08-23 and every link re-checked the same day. Where one is marked 'reversed on this route', the published profile is the other direction — the numbers describe the climb, not your day. The Col de l'Écre profile (D6, D3 and D12, topping at 1118 m) is the exact ascent line stages 7 and 8 ride.
  • Strava segments. Every stage corridor was swept with Strava's Segment Explore on 2026-08-23, authorized via your account, and a segment is only listed where at least 70% of its points lie within about 110 m of the route. Explore surfaces popular segments per area, so an obscure sprint can still be missing until someone rides it — after your first ride of a route, that activity's own effort list is the definitive hit list.
  • The maps are embedded. Basemap tiles (© OpenStreetMap contributors, © CARTO) down to street level travel inside this page, so panning and zooming work with no network at all — useful on a plateau with no signal. Dark mode restyles the same tiles.
  • The GPX navigates. Each file carries turn cues (Locus/Garmin style) plus café, drinking-water and bike-shop waypoints pulled from OpenStreetMap. Download the GPX → Strava → Maps → Create route → import, or drop it straight onto a head unit. The track is road-exact; there is no snapping step.
  • Pace numbers. The 'at your pace' estimates use your last four months on Strava — 25–27 km/h on the flat — plus half a minute per 10 m of climbing. That climbing rate is calibrated against your own hilly rides: 50.4 km with 1,107 m and 62.5 km with 1,356 m in Italy in June, and this morning's 36.1 km with 590 m. On a rental bike of unknown fit, add ten per cent and do not chase the estimate.
  • Heat and water. Late August here means 30 °C by mid-morning at sea level, and the Caussols plateau on stage 8 has neither shade nor a tap between Gourdon and Saint-Vallier. Two bottles for stages 6–8, and start early enough that the climbing is finished before noon.
  • What this book knows about you. The stage ladder, the pace estimates and the warning on stage 12 all come from your Strava history rather than a guess: 29 rides in the last four months, a median of 44.6 km, a longest of 63.9 km, and a biggest climbing day of 1,356 m. Stage 12 is flagged because it asks for more than both of those numbers at once, on a bike you may have owned for three hours.
