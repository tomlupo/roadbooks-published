Honest notes

  • Routing. Computed 2026-08-05 with BRouter's fastbike-lowtraffic profile — it prefers paved back roads over the car-optimal line. Distances and ascent come from the router's geometry and elevation model (OSM + SRTM); short in-and-out artifacts where a waypoint sat off the through-road were trimmed. Roadworks and closures it cannot know; glance at the line before you ride.
  • The maps are embedded. Basemap tiles (© OpenStreetMap contributors, © CARTO) down to street level travel inside this page, so panning and zooming works offline and nothing is fetched from the network. Dark mode restyles the same tiles.
  • Getting a route into Strava. Download the GPX → Strava → Maps → Create route → import (or komoot / Garmin Connect). The track is road-exact; no snapping step needed.
  • Segments. Every stage corridor was swept with Strava's Segment Explore (2026-08-05, authorized via your account) — Explore surfaces the popular segments per area, so an obscure sprint can still be missing until you ride it. The named climbs — including your Palhais find, 24414664 — were verified against their Strava / climbfinder pages on 2026-08-05. Flat-stage sprint segments aren't publicly indexable; after your first ride of each route, that activity's effort list is the definitive hit list.
  • Surface & traffic are audited, not assumed. Every metre carries its OpenStreetMap tags through the router; the per-stage "paved / quiet lanes / N-roads" line is computed from them. Untagged rural lanes are assumed paved. Sintra's off-pavement metres are the viewpoint approaches, flagged on the card.
  • The GPX navigates. Each file carries turn cues (Locus/Garmin style) plus café, drinking-water and bike-shop waypoints from OpenStreetMap — prompts and supply stops on your head unit, not just a line. Hillshade relief on the mountain maps: Mapzen/Terrarium DEM via AWS Open Data.
  • Wind. Summer default is N/NW and it is the terrain here. Coastal stages: ride out into it, come home with it. A windy 50 km equals a calm hilly 70.
  • Pace numbers. "At your pace" estimates use your last month on Strava — 25–27 km/h on the flat, ~90 rpm — plus half a minute per 10 m of climbing. First Montejunto trip, add a fudge.
  • Cross-checked against your ChatGPT research: its Columbeira daily (stage 4), the Palhais→Antenas classic (stage 12) and the Bombarral vineyards loop (stage 14) are in, distances and climbs measured rather than estimated. Its Torres Vedras and Abrigada starts are easy variants of 12/13 — say the word if you want them as extra stages.
